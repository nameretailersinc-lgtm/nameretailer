## Mode A: Plan (before the DB schema exists)
Produce `docs/seo-aeo-geo-implementation.md`, sections 1–4:
1. **URL architecture.** Route list for every template. Keep WordPress slugs and trailing slashes (`trailingSlash: true`). Everything is lowercase. Define how filters and pagination behave: which filter combinations get indexable, curated landing pages, and which stay `noindex,follow` or canonicalized; use self-referencing canonicals on paginated pages and no `rel=prev/next` dependence.
2. **Redirect map.** Write `docs/redirect-map.csv` with columns `old_url,new_url,status,reason,source`. Every URL in the audit CSV appears exactly once, with 301 to its survivor, 410 for junk, or `keep` for the same URL. Avoid chains and loops. Also cover WordPress system URLs: `/feed/`, `/comments/feed/`, `/wp-json/*`, `/?p=ID`, `/category/uncategorized/`, `/author/*`, `/page/N/`, `/wp-content/uploads/*` media, `/product/*`, `/cart/`, `/checkout/`, `/my-account/`.
3. **SEO data model.** List the fields the DB and admin need per content type: seoTitle, metaDescription, canonical, robots flags, ogImage, focusKeyword, faq[] items, schemaType, lastReviewedAt, author, and sources/citations. Also list site-level settings: robots.txt body, llms.txt body, verification IDs, Organization profile, sameAs[]. Also the redirect and 404-log tables.
4. **Structured-data plan.** Which JSON-LD types go on which template, and the visible content that justifies each one.

## Mode B: Implement (in the Next.js codebase)
- **Metadata.** Use `generateMetadata` on every route with a unique title and description, absolute canonical, Open Graph and Twitter cards, and a shared title template. Titles come from the DB, with safe fallbacks. `/admin` and `/design-system` get `noindex,nofollow` and are excluded from the sitemap.
- **Sitemaps.** `app/sitemap.ts` with `generateSitemaps` splits large sets. Include only indexable 200 URLs with real `lastmod`, never a build timestamp. Add a sitemap index if split.
- **robots.** `app/robots.ts` reads the admin-editable body from settings, with a sane default. Explicitly allow reputable AI crawlers (GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, CCBot) unless the owner says otherwise. Keep commerce and account paths disallowed. Point to the sitemap.
- **JSON-LD.** Typed builders go in `lib/seo/` (use `schema-dts` types), rendered by one `<JsonLd>` server component. Types: Organization (with `sameAs` only from confirmed profiles), WebSite + SearchAction (only if on-site search exists), BreadcrumbList, BlogPosting/Article, Person, FAQPage, HowTo, ItemList, Product/Offer. Emit a type only when the page visibly shows that content. **Never** emit Review or AggregateRating without real, visible, verifiable reviews. Note that Google limits FAQ and HowTo rich results. They're still valid schema and useful for other engines, but say so honestly rather than promising rich results.
- **Redirects.** The site is self-hosted on a VPS (Next.js standalone behind Caddy), not Vercel. Fixed migration redirects go in `next.config` from the CSV, generated by a script. Keep that list tight, because every entry is evaluated per request. Admin-managed redirects live in the DB and are applied in middleware/proxy, with an in-memory cached lookup that's invalidated on admin save, and no per-request DB hit on hot paths.
- **Commerce routes.** Cart, checkout, customer account and order pages are `noindex,nofollow`, excluded from the sitemap, disallowed in robots, and served with `Cache-Control: private, no-store`. Marketplace segment pages stay indexable and cacheable. Personalized bits like cart count and login state hydrate on the client, so public pages remain static/ISR. The 404 page logs misses to the DB (rate-limited) and helps the visitor recover with search, top links and nearest-slug suggestions.
- **Internal linking.** Breadcrumbs, related posts (same cluster first), pillar↔support links from `keyword-map.csv`, and tool→marketplace links. Include a build-time orphan check that fails CI when an indexable URL has zero in-content inbound links.
- **AEO templates.** Question-style H2/H3 followed by a 40–60 word direct answer, then depth. Also: key-takeaways/summary block at the top of long articles, definition box, numbered steps, comparison table and FAQ block. Each is a content-editor block with correct semantic HTML, and the FAQ block feeds FAQPage JSON-LD automatically. Speakable markup is limited or experimental; document it as such if used.
- **GEO.** `/llms.txt` and `/llms-full.txt` are generated from DB content and settings and editable in admin. Include entity signals: About, author pages with credentials, Organization schema, and consistent name, address and contact everywhere. E-E-A-T: visible author, published/updated/last-reviewed dates, cited sources, editorial policy page, contact page.
- **Images.** `next/image` with AVIF and WebP, and explicit dimensions. Alt text is required at upload and enforced in the editor; decorative images use `alt=""` explicitly.
- **Performance.** LCP < 2.5s, INP < 200ms, CLS < 0.1 on mobile. Lighthouse 95+ for Performance, SEO, Accessibility and Best Practices. No render-blocking third-party scripts. Analytics and pixels load only after consent.
- **Tests.** Unit tests for metadata builders, sitemap output, robots output, redirect matching (including trailing-slash and case normalization) and JSON-LD validity. Playwright covers sitemap, a sample of redirects from the CSV, and 404 logging.

## Hard rules
- **GEO honesty.** State plainly that no AI engine publishes ranking factors. Label every GEO tactic as *best practice* (evidence-backed or low-risk) or *experimental*. llms.txt is an emerging convention, not a confirmed ranking signal.
- **Facts.** Never invent facts, authors, credentials, stats, reviews or social profiles. Missing data becomes a clearly marked placeholder plus an entry in your report's "needs owner input" list.
- **Rankings.** Don't break existing rankings for tidiness. Change a slug only when the gain clearly outweighs the migration risk, and explain why in the redirect CSV `reason` column.
