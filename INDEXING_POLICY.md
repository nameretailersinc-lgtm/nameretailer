# Crawl and indexing policy

The canonical home is `/`. Catalogue filter, sort, search and nonstandard page-size URLs use `noindex,follow` and the clean canonical. Robots blocks these query keys to limit crawl growth; blocked URLs must first be crawled without that restriction if Google needs to observe a new noindex directive. Empty pages remain noindex. Clean, populated pagination is crawlable and self-canonical, including blog pagination; page 1 canonicalizes to the clean URL.

Every active, committed publication may have an internal detail page. Index eligibility is separate: a valid root domain/name, specific niche, nonempty country and language, a positive integer-cent USD price, at least 80 words of supplied publication requirements with 40 distinct tokens, and unique normalized requirements among candidate profiles. The existing stricter supplied-metric gate is retained: DA >=50, DR >=60, estimated traffic >=500,000, spam score <=10. Section listings, ambiguous duplicate paths, thin and duplicate profiles remain noindex,follow. Metadata and sitemap membership use the same cached eligible set. This is a data-completeness rule, not an independent endorsement. Add real publication-specific descriptions in the existing requirements field; never pad generic text to pass it.

Sitemap index `/sitemap.xml` references separate static, hub, guide/article and qualified-publication files. Each part is capped at 20,000 URLs and 45 MiB (below the protocol limits of 50,000 and 50 MB). Empty groups have no part. Real record update times supply lastmod; undated static pages omit it. Candidate profiles and sitemap snapshots refresh after 15 minutes, with no stale-on-error profile eligibility. Private routes, aliases, facets, unfinished legal pages and thin hubs are excluded. All published blog articles are included under the owner approval below.

Hub eligibility requires at least 15 active listings and substantive distinct guidance. Metric bands have inclusive non-overlapping integer boundaries; USD bands use integer cents. The broad above-500,000 traffic comparison is a separate threshold view. AI crawler rules follow the same public/private boundaries. No Product, Offer or rating markup describes unavailable publication ordering.
